## 0. How a retrieval assistant should use this document

1. **This is practical guidance, not law.** Statutory text, Rules and notifications control. Ministry FAQs and handbooks are explanatory only; the Ministry's own documents say the Codes prevail if there is a conflict.
2. **Never apply a module before the applicability check** in REF-01. Many wage rights depend on who the employer is, whether the establishment is Central or State sphere, and whether the person is an "employee".
3. **Claim IDs** in brackets (for example `[WAGE-011]`) point to the source register. **UNRES-xx** points to open questions. Where an `UNRES` item applies, say plainly that the point is unsettled. Do not pick a side silently.
4. Do not draft legal notices, complaints or applications. Explain the situation, say what to check, and suggest next steps.
5. Ask for missing facts one or two at a time, not as a long form.
6. Quotations from legislation are deliberately short fragments. Check the full provision before relying on any wording.

## REF-01: Applicability triage (ask this before any wage advice)

```yaml
document_id: employment_wage_practical_guide
module_id: REF-01
topic: Applicability triage – who is covered by the Code on Wages wage-payment rules
jurisdiction: India – Central and State
relevant_sections: [CoW s.2(d), s.2(k), s.2(l), s.2(m), s.2(o), s.25, s.43; SS Code s.2(35), s.2(60), s.2(61); OSH Code s.1(3), s.2(1)(zzl)]
applicable_acts: [Code on Wages, 2019; Code on Social Security, 2020; Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020]
source_references: [WAGE-004, WAGE-005, WAGE-006, WAGE-007, WAGE-014, WAGE-015, APPLIC-001, APPLIC-002, APPLIC-003]
authority_level: non_authoritative_practical_guidance
review_status: independent_research_pending_review
last_verified_date: 2026-11-08
```

### A. Situation overview
The same message ("my salary is late") can lead to different law depending on the person's status. Ask the questions below before giving specific steps.

### B1. Legal quotations (fragments only)
- Employee excludes an apprentice "engaged under the Apprentices Act, 1961". [WAGE-004]
- Chapter III "shall not apply to the Government establishments" unless notified. [WAGE-014]
- Government establishment means "any office or department of the Government or a local authority". [WAGE-007]

### B2. Legal explanation
| Situation | Effect on Code on Wages Chapter III (timely payment, deductions) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Private company, firm, shop, LLP, trust employee on wages | Covered. Appropriate Government is usually the **State**, unless the establishment is in a listed Central category (see below). | [WAGE-005] |
| Employee of a Central-sphere establishment (railways, mines, oilfield, major ports, air transport, telecommunication, banking, insurance company, Central corporations, Central PSUs and their subsidiaries, Central autonomous bodies, and **their contractors' establishments**) | Covered. Appropriate Government is the **Central Government**. This includes many **private** employers in telecom, aviation, banking and mining. | [WAGE-005] |
| Employee of a Central or State Government **office or department**, or a local authority | **Chapter III (ss.15–25, including s.17 and s.18) does not apply** unless the appropriate Government has notified it. No such notification was found (UNRES-09). Service rules apply instead. Section 25 exempts only "this Chapter", so it does not by itself remove the overtime provision (s.14, Chapter II). | [WAGE-014] |
| Public-sector **undertaking** (a company or corporation) | Not a "Government establishment" as defined (office or department). Chapter III applies, and the Central Government is the appropriate Government for Central PSUs. | [WAGE-005, WAGE-007] |
| Apprentice engaged under the Apprentices Act, 1961 | Not an "employee". The Code's wage-payment rules do not apply. A stipend dispute lies under that Act (not researched). | [WAGE-004] |
| Trainee, intern or "management trainee" **not** engaged under the Apprentices Act | Covered if employed on wages. The label does not decide it. | [WAGE-004] |
| Contractual / fixed-term employee paid by the establishment | Covered as an employee. | [WAGE-004] |
| Employee **of a contractor** (contract labour) | Covered. The contractor is an "employer". If the contractor fails to pay, the principal employer (the company or firm that is proprietor of the establishment) is responsible. | [WAGE-006, WAGE-015] |
| Gig or platform worker | The Social Security Code defines gig and platform workers as people earning **outside** a traditional employer–employee relationship. They are not automatically "employees" under the Code on Wages. Whether a particular person is really an employee is a fact-and-law question (UNRES-17). | [APPLIC-002] |
| Independent contractor or consultant (own business, invoices, no control) | Not an employee. The Code does not apply. Contract and civil remedies apply. | [WAGE-004] |
| Managerial or supervisory staff | The definition of **employee** expressly includes managerial, supervisory and administrative work, so Chapter III applies. (The narrower definition of **worker**, which excludes mainly managerial staff and some supervisors, matters mostly for the IR Code and OSH Code.) | [WAGE-004, WAGE-022] |
| Member of the Armed Forces | Excluded from "employee". | [WAGE-004] |

### C. Questions to ask (in this order, one or two at a time)
1. Who is the employer, and is it a company, firm, shop, a Government office or a contractor?
2. Is the work in railways, mines, ports, aviation, telecom, banking or insurance, or for a Central PSU?
3. Are you on the employer's payroll with a salary, or paid per task or invoice?
4. Which state do you work in?
5. Were you an apprentice under the Apprentices Act?

### F. Exceptions and limitations
- The Central/State classification follows the **establishment**, not where the head office is. Check the definition before assuming. [WAGE-005]
- "Banking and insurance company" is worded broadly in the Code on Wages definition; see UNRES-10 for the unresolved point.

## REF-02: Meaning of "wages" (and why CTC is not the wage base)

```yaml
document_id: employment_wage_practical_guide
module_id: REF-02
topic: Statutory meaning of wages; salary vs wages vs CTC
jurisdiction: India – Central and State (definition is in the Code itself)
relevant_sections: [CoW s.2(y); SS Code s.2(88); OSH Code s.2(1)(zzj)]
applicable_acts: [Code on Wages, 2019]
source_references: [WAGE-008, WAGE-009, SETTLEMENT-006, SETTLEMENT-012]
authority_level: non_authoritative_practical_guidance
review_status: independent_research_pending_review
last_verified_date: 2026-11-08
```

### B1. Legal quotations (fragments)
- Wages means "all remuneration whether by way of salaries, allowances or otherwise". [WAGE-008]
- It "includes" basic pay, dearness allowance and retaining allowance. [WAGE-008]

### B2. Legal explanation
**What is in:** basic pay, dearness allowance, retaining allowance (if any), and any other remuneration payable under the terms of employment, **subject to the exclusions and the 50% rule below**. [WAGE-008]

**Listed exclusions (items (a)–(k)):** statutory bonus not forming part of contractual remuneration; value of housing and amenities; employer PF/pension contributions; conveyance allowance; special expenses to do the job; house rent allowance; remuneration under an award or settlement; overtime allowance; commission; gratuity; retrenchment compensation, retirement benefits and ex gratia on termination. [WAGE-008]

**The 50% add-back mechanism:** if the payments under exclusions (a) to (i) exceed one-half (or a different percentage notified by the Central Government) of **all remuneration**, the excess is added back and counted as wages. [WAGE-008]

**Second proviso (important for pay disputes):** "for the purpose of payment of wages", the items at (d) conveyance allowance, (f) house rent allowance, (g) award or settlement remuneration and (h) overtime allowance are **taken into account** in computing wages. So unpaid HRA or conveyance allowance is not ignored just because it is outside the basic definition. [WAGE-009]

**Salary, wages and CTC:**
- *Salary* is a contract word. *Wages* is the statutory term. They overlap but are not identical.
- *CTC* usually includes employer PF contributions, gratuity provisions, insurance, bonus and benefits that are not paid to the employee. Several of those are expressly excluded from wages.
- Do not compute overtime, gratuity or other statutory sums from CTC. Use the wage figure as defined for that statute, taken from payslips.
- Ministry FAQs (March 2026) say annual performance-based incentives are not part of wages, and that overtime allowance counts within the exclusion bucket for the 50% test. These FAQs are explanatory, not law. [SETTLEMENT-012]
- Gratuity is computed on "wages" as defined in the **Social Security Code** (s.2(88)), not the Code on Wages. The Ministry FAQ says the revised wage definition applies from 21 November 2025. [SETTLEMENT-006]

### C. What the employee should do
Collect three recent payslips and the appointment letter. Identify basic, DA, HRA, conveyance allowance, special allowance, variable pay, and employer-side items that never reach the bank account. Ask the user which of these they are disputing.

### F. Exceptions and limitations
- Whether a given allowance (for example "special allowance") is "wages" depends on its nature and terms. Not every label is settled (UNRES-05).
- Payments "in kind" are counted only up to fifteen per cent of total wages. [WAGE-009]

## REF-03: Central versus State rules

```yaml
document_id: employment_wage_practical_guide
module_id: REF-03
topic: Which Rules apply – Central Rules or State Rules
jurisdiction: India – Central and State
relevant_sections: [CoW s.2(d), s.67(1), s.45(1), s.49(1), s.51(1)]
applicable_acts: [Code on Wages, 2019; Code on Wages (Central) Rules, 2026 (G.S.R. 343(E))]
source_references: [WAGE-005, WAGE-020, APPLIC-001, REMEDY-001, REMEDY-007]
authority_level: non_authoritative_practical_guidance
review_status: independent_research_pending_review
last_verified_date: 2026-11-08
```

### B2. Legal explanation
- The Code applies across India. [WAGE-002] The **appropriate Government** makes rules and appoints the claims authority, appellate authority and Inspector-cum-Facilitators for establishments in its sphere. [WAGE-005, APPLIC-001]
- The Central Rules (G.S.R. 343(E), 8 May 2026, in force on publication) define "authority" and "appellate authority" as those appointed by the **Central Government**. They therefore govern **Central-sphere** establishments only. [WAGE-020]
- For most private-sector employees, the **State Government** is the appropriate Government and **State Rules** govern procedure (claims authority, fine procedure, advance recovery, forms). The status of State Rules is uneven and trackers conflict (UNRES-10).
- The **statute itself** (for example the s.17 deadlines, the s.18 list of permitted deductions, the 50% cap, the s.45 three-year limit) applies in both spheres. Central Rules details (for example Rule 13 carry-forward, Rule 14 approving authority for fines, the Form II claim format) should not be quoted to a State-sphere employee as if they were binding on that employer.
- Ministry FAQ (March 2026, Q27, an OSH health-check example) states the general principle that Central rules apply where the Central Government is appropriate Government and State rules where the State Government is. [APPLIC-001]

### C. What the assistant should say when State Rules are needed
"The core deadlines in the Code apply everywhere. The exact filing process depends on your state's rules, which I can't confirm for your state. Your state labour department's website, or the notice board at your workplace (which must name your Inspector-cum-Facilitator), is the safest place to confirm."

### F. Unverified state-specific points
State claims-authority designation, State forms and fees, State fine and deduction procedures, any State-specific s.17(3) time limits and State overtime rules. See UNRES-10.

## REF-04: The remedy routes are NOT interchangeable

```yaml

relevant_sections: [CoW s.45, s.49, s.51, s.52, s.54, s.57, s.59; IR Code s.4 (Handbook only)]
applicable_acts: [Code on Wages, 2019; Industrial Relations Code, 2020 (Handbook reference only)]
source_references: [REMEDY-001 to REMEDY-016, PORTAL-001 to PORTAL-003]
authority_level: non_authoritative_practical_guidance
review_status: independent_research_pending_review
last_verified_date: 2026-11-08
```

| Route | What it is for | Conditions and notes | Binding outcome? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Written request to employer** (HR / payroll / manager) | Fastest practical fix and the paper trail. | No statutory requirement to do this first. | No. |
| **Internal grievance** (employer's own process; for some workers a statutory Grievance Redressal Committee) | Resolve inside the company. | The IR Code's committee applies to *industrial establishments with 20 or more workers*, with a one-year filing window and 30-day disposal, per the Ministry Handbook only (UNRES-16). **Not** a pre-condition to a Code on Wages claim (no such condition in s.45). | No (recommendatory). |
| **Claim under the Code (s.45)** | Money claim for unpaid wages, wrongful deductions, overtime and other Code dues. | Filed before the authority appointed by the appropriate Government, by the employee, a registered trade union the employee belongs to, or the Inspector-cum-Facilitator. **Limitation: three years** from when the claim arose, extendable on sufficient cause. | **Yes.** Can award the claim plus compensation up to ten times the claim. Recoverable as arrears of land revenue. |
| **Inspector-cum-Facilitator** | Advice, inspection, examining registers, seizing records, enforcement and prosecution. | Appointed by the appropriate Government. Name and address must appear on the workplace notice board. Not a court. | Indirect. Can enforce compliance and initiate prosecution. |
| **Government grievance portal** (SAMADHAN 2.0) | Lodging a labour grievance or claim online with the labour administration. | Official platform for Labour Code matters since 2026. State-sphere coverage unresolved (UNRES-11). Old SAMADHAN is **not** for fresh Labour Code filings. | Depends on what is filed and which authority picks it up. |
| **Court or other legal proceeding** | Prosecution of offences; writ petitions (for example for Government employees); civil claims for non-Code dues. | A civil suit for sums recoverable under the Code (minimum wages, deductions, bonus) is barred where it could have been recovered under the Code (s.57). Offences: complaint by employee, union or Inspector-cum-Facilitator. Needs a lawyer in practice. | Yes, after trial. Slower. |

**Do not tell users to "exhaust internal procedures first" as if required.** The Code does not make internal escalation a precondition to a s.45 claim. Internal steps help in practice, but legal deadlines (especially three years) keep running. [REMEDY-005]

## PRACTICAL-01: Delayed Monthly Salary

```yaml
document_id: employment_wage_practical_guide
module_id: PRACTICAL-01
topic: Delayed monthly salary
jurisdiction: India – Central and State (statutory deadlines are common; procedure differs)
relevant_sections: [CoW s.15, s.16, s.17(1), s.17(3), s.17(4), s.25, s.43, s.45, s.50, s.52, s.54]
applicable_acts: [Code on Wages, 2019; Code on Wages (Central) Rules, 2026 (Central sphere only)]
source_references: [WAGE-001, WAGE-002, WAGE-003, WAGE-010, WAGE-011, WAGE-013, WAGE-014, WAGE-015, WAGE-016, WAGE-019, REMEDY-005, REMEDY-010, REMEDY-011]
authority_level: non_authoritative_practical_guidance
review_status: independent_research_pending_review
last_verified_date: 2026-11-08
```

### A. Situation overview
Salary arrives late, arrives in part, or does not arrive. The employee wants to know whether this is unlawful and what to do. First separate three cases: (1) paid **late**, (2) **short-paid**, (3) **not paid at all**. They are handled differently.

### B1. Legal quotations (fragments)
- Monthly wages: "before the expiry of the seventh day of the succeeding month". [WAGE-011]
- Wage period: "no wage period ... shall be more than a month". [WAGE-010]
- Chapter III "shall not apply to the Government establishments" unless notified. [WAGE-014]

### B2. Legal explanation
1. **Deadline by wage period (s.17(1)).** Daily wage: at the end of the shift. Weekly: on the last working day of the week, before the weekly holiday. Fortnightly: before the end of the second day after the fortnight. **Monthly: before the seventh day of the following month expires.** For example, March wages must be paid by 7 April. [WAGE-011]
2. **Wage period is fixed by the employer** (daily, weekly, fortnightly or monthly), never longer than one month. [WAGE-010]
3. **Applies to all employees regardless of salary level.** The Statement of Objects says the old ₹24,000 ceiling was removed. The Ministry FAQ (March 2026, Q2) confirms the provisions apply to all employees. This is explanatory material; the section text has no wage ceiling. [WAGE-011, WAGE-019]
4. **Other time limits.** The appropriate Government may fix a different time limit (s.17(3)), and other laws' time limits are not affected (s.17(4)). No State-specific variation was verified (UNRES-10). [WAGE-013]
5. **Mode of payment.** Cash, cheque, bank credit or electronic mode. [WAGE-003 area; India Code s.15]
6. **Employer responsibility.** The "employer" includes a contractor. If the employer fails, the proprietor of the establishment is responsible (s.43). [WAGE-015]
7. **Consequences (my reading, see UNRES-13).** A breach of s.17 is a contravention that can attract a fine under s.54 (general contravention: up to ₹20,000; repeat within five years can carry imprisonment up to one month or a fine of up to ₹40,000, or both). **Underpayment** of the amount due is punishable by a fine of up to ₹50,000, rising for repeats. A complaint may be made by the employee. Before prosecuting for a general contravention, the Inspector-cum-Facilitator must give the employer a written chance to comply. [REMEDY-010, REMEDY-011]
8. **Claim.** Unpaid wages can be claimed under s.45 within three years. Whether s.45 compensation is available where wages were eventually paid late (not unpaid) is unsettled (UNRES-13). [REMEDY-005]

### C. What should the employee do? (chronological)
1. **Identify exactly what is missing.** Which month, gross or net, all or part? Compare with the payslip and the pay date in the appointment letter.
2. **Check applicability** (REF-01). Government department, apprentice, contractor-paid, or platform arrangement changes the answer.
3. **Do the calendar.** Find the wage period. For monthly pay, the outer statutory date is the 7th of the next month. Note that a contractual pay date earlier than the 7th is a contract promise; the statutory limit is the 7th.
4. **Check for a lawful reason for any shortfall** (advance recovery, absence, tax and so on). See PRACTICAL-04.
5. **Preserve evidence** (section D).
6. **Raise it in writing with HR or payroll.** Keep it short and factual: what is missing, when it was due, and ask for a specific date. This is the practical step. It is not legally required before other routes.
7. **Record the reply.** Note dates, names and promises. After any call, send a one-line email confirming what was said.
8. **Choose a statutory route if it is not fixed** (REF-04): the Inspector-cum-Facilitator named on the workplace notice board; SAMADHAN 2.0; a s.45 claim; or a lawyer if the amount or pattern is serious.
9. **Safe practice.** Keep copies of **your own** payslips, bank statements and emails. Do not copy confidential company data, access systems you are no longer authorised to use, or post accusations publicly. Do not resign or stop work purely because of a delay without first understanding notice-period consequences (consider PRACTICAL-02 and a short legal consult).

### D. Evidence to preserve
- Appointment or offer letter (and any pay-date clause).
- Payslips for several months, showing the usual credit pattern. Employers must issue wage slips on or before payment of wages. [WAGE-016]
- Bank statements showing credit dates and amounts.
- HR or payroll emails and messages acknowledging the delay or giving a new date.
- Attendance proof or timesheets for the unpaid period.
- A note of the wage period and pay date displayed at the workplace (employers must display wage period and payment date). [WAGE-016]

### E. Possible escalation routes
Written follow-up with HR → (where applicable) internal grievance committee → Inspector-cum-Facilitator → SAMADHAN 2.0 or a s.45 claim to the notified authority → lawyer. See REF-04 for what each is for. In the Central sphere, the s.45 authority is a Regional Labour Commissioner (Central) (REMEDY-007; see UNRES-12).

### F. Exceptions and limitations
- Not covered: Government departments and local authorities (unless Chapter III is notified), apprentices, armed forces, independent contractors.
- A State may set another time limit under s.17(3). Not verified for any State.
- "Variable pay", "performance bonus" and similar may not be "wages" at all (REF-02). Delay in those is judged on the contract.
- Whether a holiday shifts the 7th-day limit is not addressed in the Code text.

### G. When to consult a lawyer
Repeated or growing arrears; employer insolvent, closed or not responding; adverse action after complaining; a request to sign a release; misclassification (you are treated as a contractor or gig worker but work like an employee); Government or PSU service rules; you are considering resigning because of non-payment.

### H. Example conversational response
> "Under the Code on Wages, a monthly-paid employee's salary has to be paid before the 7th day of the next month runs out. So March salary is due by 7 April. That applies at any salary level, with a few exceptions, such as Government departments and apprentices.
>
> To tell you what applies to you, can you tell me who your employer is (a company, a Government office, or a contractor), and how late the salary is?
>
> In the meantime, save your last few payslips and bank statements, and send HR a short email saying which month is unpaid and asking for a date. If it keeps slipping, there are official routes, such as the Inspector-cum-Facilitator at your workplace or the labour grievance portal. I can walk you through which fits your case."

## PRACTICAL-02: Unpaid Salary After Resignation

```yaml
document_id: employment_wage_practical_guide
module_id: PRACTICAL-02
topic: Unpaid earned salary after resignation (and after dismissal, retrenchment, closure)
jurisdiction: India – Central and State
relevant_sections: [CoW s.2(y), s.17(2), s.17(3), s.18, s.20, s.21, s.45, s.59, s.60; OSH Code s.32(1)(vi); SS Code s.53, s.56]
applicable_acts: [Code on Wages, 2019; OSH Code, 2020 (leave wages, "workers"); Code on Social Security, 2020 (gratuity)]
source_references: [WAGE-008, WAGE-009, WAGE-012, WAGE-013, WAGE-017, WAGE-018, DEDUCTION-001, DEDUCTION-002, DEDUCTION-003, SETTLEMENT-001, SETTLEMENT-005, SETTLEMENT-009]
authority_level: non_authoritative_practical_guidance
review_status: independent_research_pending_review
last_verified_date: 2026-11-08
```

### A. Situation overview
The employee has resigned (or been removed) and the employer has not paid earned salary. Often the employer says "full and final settlement is processed after clearance". The key distinction: **earned wages** versus the wider **settlement bundle** (gratuity, leave, bonus, reimbursements). They do not share one deadline.

### B1. Legal quotations (fragments)
- Where an employee has been "removed or dismissed", "retrenched or has resigned", or "became unemployed due to closure". [WAGE-012]
- The wages payable "shall be paid within two working days". [WAGE-012]
- Any agreement giving up Code dues "shall be null and void" to that extent. [WAGE-018]

### B2. Legal explanation
1. **Section 17(2)** requires wages payable to be paid within **two working days** after: removal or dismissal; retrenchment; resignation; or becoming unemployed because the establishment closed. [WAGE-012]
2. **Which wages.** The sub-section refers to "the wages payable". This covers earned wages as defined in s.2(y), including allowances that count for payment of wages (REF-02). The text of s.17(2) does **not** mention gratuity, bonus, notice pay, reimbursements or "full and final settlement". The Code does not use the phrase "full and final settlement". [WAGE-012, SETTLEMENT-001]
3. **When does the clock start?** The text says "of ... his resignation". It does not say whether that is the date the resignation is given, accepted, or the last working day. "Working day" is not defined in the Code or the Central Rules provisions I read. The commonly used safe reading is the date employment actually ends, but that is an interpretation (UNRES-03).
4. **Events not listed.** Section 17(2) does not name expiry of a fixed-term contract, retirement or death. The deadline for those is unsettled (UNRES-04).
5. **No clearance exception in the text.** Nothing in s.17(2) makes payment conditional on exit formalities. The appropriate Government may provide "any other time limit" (s.17(3)); no such notification was found (UNRES-10). [WAGE-012, WAGE-013]
6. **What an employer may deduct.** Only deductions authorised by s.18(2) are allowed. The authorised heads include recovery of advances and **adjustment of overpayment of wages**, income tax, PF and similar contributions, and, with procedure, damage or loss of goods entrusted to the employee. The Code text does not list "notice-period shortfall" as an authorised head (UNRES-06). A contract clause cannot authorise a deduction the Code does not authorise. [DEDUCTION-001, DEDUCTION-002]
7. **50% cap.** Total deductions in a wage period may not exceed 50% of wages; excess is recovered later in instalments. How that works at the **final** pay, when there is no later wage period, is unsettled (UNRES-07). [DEDUCTION-003]
8. **Waiver.** A contract or agreement by which an employee gives up an amount due under the Code is void to that extent. This is relevant to "no-dues" or release forms. It does not decide non-Code claims (UNRES-22). [WAGE-018]
9. **Leave wages (for "workers" under the OSH Code).** The OSH Code requires wages in lieu of accrued annual leave when a worker quits, and requires payment before the expiry of the second working day after quitting. It covers "workers" only (excludes mainly managerial staff and supervisors earning above ₹18,000). Others depend on contract or State law (UNRES-27). [SETTLEMENT-009]
10. **Burden of proof.** Once a claim is filed for non-payment or unauthorised deduction, the employer must prove it has paid. [WAGE-017]

### C. What should the employee do?
1. **List precisely what is missing.** Earned salary for days worked up to the last day, allowances, overtime, any wrongly deducted amount. Separate from gratuity, leave, bonus and reimbursements (PRACTICAL-06).
2. **Confirm key dates:** date resignation was given, date accepted, last working day, relieving date.
3. **Check applicability** (REF-01), and whether you are a "worker" for OSH leave wages.
4. **Ask for the F&F working in writing** with a component-wise statement, including any recoveries and the reason for each.
5. **Preserve evidence** (D).
6. **Raise it in writing** with HR or payroll, quoting dates. Say which items are earned wages and ask for the payment date.
7. **If the employer cites incomplete clearance:** ask in writing which item is outstanding and what loss they claim. Keep the reply. Withholding all earned wages is not supported by the s.17(2) text; any deduction must fit s.18 and its procedure (show-cause for damage or loss).
8. **Read any release form before signing.** Ask what each line gives up. If payment is conditioned on signing a full release, get advice first.
9. **Escalate if unpaid** (REF-04): Inspector-cum-Facilitator, SAMADHAN 2.0 or a s.45 claim (three years), lawyer.
10. **Safe practice.** Return company property in a documented way (email with a list and date). Do not retain or copy confidential data. Keep a record of the handover.

### D. Evidence to preserve
- Resignation email and acceptance (or non-response), with dates.
- Last working day proof, relieving or experience letter, exit checklist.
- Final three payslips; attendance and leave balance statements.
- Appointment letter and policy on notice period and recoveries.
- The F&F statement, any calculation emailed to you, and messages giving dates for payment.
- Bank statements showing the last credit.
- Handover or asset-return acknowledgement.

### E. Possible escalation routes
HR/payroll in writing → Inspector-cum-Facilitator → SAMADHAN 2.0 → s.45 claim before the notified authority (Central sphere: Regional Labour Commissioner (Central)) → lawyer. Offence complaints under s.52 are available. A civil suit for sums recoverable under the Code is barred by s.57. [REMEDY-004 to REMEDY-013]

### F. Exceptions and limitations
- Government departments (Chapter III exempt unless notified), apprentices, gig or independent contractors: s.17(2) may not apply.
- Fixed-term expiry, retirement, death: not named in s.17(2) (UNRES-04).
- The start date of the two working days is unsettled (UNRES-03).
- Whether leave encashment, commission and reimbursements are "wages payable" under s.17(2) is unsettled except for OSH "workers", where s.32(1)(vi) gives its own deadline (UNRES-05).
- Notice-pay recovery and the 50% cap at exit are unsettled (UNRES-06, UNRES-07).

### G. When to consult a lawyer
Employer demands notice-pay recovery that is large; a no-dues release is a condition of payment; you are a contractual or fixed-term employee with a bond; employer disputes you ever resigned or says you absconded; sum is substantial; you suspect fraud or forged documents; Government or PSU employee.

### H. Example conversational response
> "I'm sorry you're dealing with this. Under the Code on Wages, the salary you've already earned has to be paid within two working days after you resign or leave. That timeline is about earned wages, which means salary for days worked and allowances that count as wages. Things like gratuity, bonus and reimbursements follow different rules, so they shouldn't be lumped together with it.
>
> Two quick things so I can be specific: what was your last working day, and what is still unpaid, just the salary or other things as well?
>
> If they're saying it's held until clearance, ask them in writing which item is pending. I can't find anything in the Code that lets an employer hold earned salary back for that reason, though they can make some limited, lawful deductions. Save your resignation email, last payslip and any messages about the F&F date, and avoid signing a 'no claims' form until you've read what it gives up."

## PRACTICAL-03: Unpaid Overtime

```yaml
document_id: employment_wage_practical_guide
module_id: PRACTICAL-03
topic: Unpaid overtime
jurisdiction: India – Central and State (hours and overtime triggers differ by appropriate Government and by Code)
relevant_sections: [CoW s.2(y), s.13, s.14, s.25 (not applicable to s.14); Central Rules r.2(1)(p), r.5, r.6, r.8, r.9, r.51; OSH Code s.2(1)(zzl), s.25, s.26, s.27]
applicable_acts: [Code on Wages, 2019; Code on Wages (Central) Rules, 2026; OSH Code, 2020]
source_references: [OVERTIME-001 to OVERTIME-010, WAGE-008, WAGE-009, WAGE-016, REMEDY-004, REMEDY-005, REMEDY-016]
authority_level: non_authoritative_practical_guidance
review_status: independent_research_pending_review
last_verified_date: 2026-11-08
```

### A. Situation overview
The employee worked beyond normal hours (or on a rest day) and was not paid extra, or was paid at a flat rate. Two statutes touch overtime, with **different coverage**, and the hour-limits depend on the appropriate Government. Do not assume "8 hours a day, 48 hours a week" for everyone.

### B1. Legal quotations (fragments)
- Section 14: pay "at the overtime rate which shall not be less than twice the normal rate". [OVERTIME-001]
- OSH Code s.27: overtime "subject to the consent of such worker". [OVERTIME-008]
- OSH Code s.25(1): "eight hours in a day". [OVERTIME-007]

### B2. Legal explanation
1. **Code on Wages s.14.** If an **employee** whose minimum rate of wages has been fixed under the Code by the hour, by the day or by a longer prescribed wage period works beyond the hours of a normal working day, the employer must pay for every hour **or part of an hour** at **not less than twice the normal rate of wages**. [OVERTIME-001]
   - In the Central Rules, the "longer wage period" is **the month**. [OVERTIME-002]
   - Whether s.14 reaches employees paid well above minimum wage is not clear from the text alone. The Ministry FAQ (March 2026, Q5) says an *employee, including a worker, whose minimum rate of wages is fixed under the Code* is eligible for overtime, including supervisory and managerial staff. This is explanatory, not binding (UNRES-08). [OVERTIME-010]
2. **OSH Code s.25 and s.27.** For **workers** in establishments covered by the OSH Code: no more than eight hours a day (with intervals and spread-over as notified); overtime is paid at **twice the rate of wages** where a worker works beyond the hours prescribed by the appropriate Government in a day or a week, calculated on a daily or weekly basis **whichever is more favourable**; overtime requires the worker's **consent**; and the government may prescribe a cap on total overtime hours. [OVERTIME-007, OVERTIME-008]
   - "Worker" excludes people employed mainly in a managerial or administrative capacity and supervisors drawing more than ₹18,000 a month. [OVERTIME-009]
   - The OSH Code does not apply to offices of the Central or State Government. [OVERTIME-009]
3. **Normal hours under the Central Rules (Central sphere only).** Daily-wage employees: eight hours. Employees on other wage periods: hours fixed so that **weekly hours do not exceed 48**. [OVERTIME-003]
4. **Not everyone has the same limits.** Employees in emergencies, preparatory or complementary work, intermittent work, work that must be finished for technical reasons, or work dependent on natural forces fall under s.13(2). For them, the working-hour provisions apply only to the extent prescribed. The Central Rules say their hours may exceed normal hours, **but overtime is still payable under s.14**. [OVERTIME-004]
5. **Rest-day work (Central Rules).** An employee should not work on the rest day unless a substitute rest day is allowed. If they do, the rest-day work is paid at the overtime rate. No more than ten consecutive days without a rest day. [OVERTIME-005]
6. **Normal rate of wages** means wages as defined in s.2(y). The extracted rule text is partly illegible (UNRES-02). [OVERTIME-006]
7. **Government establishments.** Section 25 exempts only Chapter III. Overtime under s.14 is in Chapter II, so s.25 does not itself remove it. In practice, the Central Rules say the Central Government will not fix minimum wages for Central Government employees, so the s.14 condition ("minimum rate of wages fixed") may not be met (UNRES-09). [WAGE-014, APPLIC-004]
8. **State laws.** State Shops and Establishments laws or State OSH Rules may set different hours and overtime caps. Not verified (UNRES-08, UNRES-10).

### Illustrative arithmetic (not a legal formula)
If wages as defined are ₹40,000 a month, a common conversion is ÷ 26 days ÷ 8 hours = ₹192.31 an hour. Double that is ₹384.62. Ten overtime hours would be about ₹3,846. The Central Rules use "÷ 26" and "÷ 8" for converting minimum-wage rates; whether that exact method is mandatory for salaried overtime is not stated (UNRES-08). Never calculate from CTC (REF-02).

### C. What should the employee do?
1. **Identify the gap:** which dates, how many hours, what was paid for them.
2. **Check applicability:** employee or worker? Central or State sphere? Government office? Managerial or supervisory? (REF-01)
3. **Read your contract and payslips** for an hours clause and any overtime line. Compare with what the employer's records say.
4. **Reconstruct the hours** from your own log (see D), starting from the daily and weekly limit that applies. Ask the user: was the overtime asked for or approved, and how many hours a week is the normal schedule?
5. **Calculate a rough claim** from wages as defined, not CTC (illustration above). Say it is an estimate.
6. **Raise it in writing** with the dates and hours, and ask for the payroll's overtime record.
7. **Record the response.** If told "overtime is included in your salary," note it and see section F.
8. **Escalate** if needed (E). Seek a lawyer for large or multi-year claims.
9. **Safe practice:** log hours honestly from your own records. Do not copy confidential logs or screenshots of internal systems beyond what you need to show your own hours.

### D. Evidence to preserve
- Your own contemporaneous log: date, start, end, breaks, task.
- Email, chat and ticket timestamps, calendar invites, VPN or access logs you are entitled to hold.
- Rosters or shift schedules; written requests from managers to stay late or work on rest days.
- Payslips (overtime line), offer letter hours clause.
- The employer's attendance register-cum-muster roll and wage register contain overtime columns and the wage slip has an overtime line; ask for your own entries. [OVERTIME-010, WAGE-016]

### E. Possible escalation routes
HR/payroll in writing → Inspector-cum-Facilitator → SAMADHAN 2.0 (the portal lists non-payment of overtime) → s.45 claim (Form II in the Central Rules has an overtime-rate head; three years) → lawyer. Once a claim is filed, the employer carries the burden to prove payment, but the employee still needs to show the hours worked. [REMEDY-004, REMEDY-005, REMEDY-016, WAGE-017]

### F. Exceptions and limitations
- **No single universal rule.** Hour limits, overtime triggers and caps differ between the Code on Wages, the OSH Code, Central Rules and State rules.
- s.14 is tied to "minimum rate of wages fixed" (UNRES-08).
- OSH Code protection is for "workers"; managers and higher-paid supervisors are outside it.
- Consent and unauthorised overtime: the text requires consent to work overtime, but does not say whether overtime performed without approval is unpaid (UNRES-08).
- Reported OSH Central Rules overtime cap (for example 144 hours a quarter) comes only from secondary sources (UNRES-08).
- "Overtime is already included in your CTC" is not a recognised exception in the sections read. s.60 voids agreements that remove the Code liability to pay.

### G. When to consult a lawyer
Claim covers months or years; you are managerial or supervisory and the employer says you are excluded; shift-based, factory or contract-labour settings; the employer says overtime was voluntary; Government or PSU service rules.

### H. Example conversational response
> "Overtime is paid at not less than double the normal rate for each extra hour. How it applies depends on who you are and where you work, so I'll ask a couple of things first: are you in a private company, a Government office or a contractor's setup, and is your role managerial, supervisory or regular staff? Roughly how many hours a week is your normal schedule?
>
> Start keeping a simple log of dates and start and finish times, and save emails or chats where you were asked to stay late. When you work out what you're owed, use the wages shown on your payslip (basic, DA and the like), not your CTC. If the employer says it's 'included in the package', ask them to show where that is written. If you want, I can talk through your options, from a written request to HR up to an official claim."

## PRACTICAL-04: Salary Deductions

```yaml
document_id: employment_wage_practical_guide
module_id: PRACTICAL-04
topic: Unauthorised salary deductions vs lawful adjustments
jurisdiction: India – Central and State (list of heads is statutory; procedure rules differ)
relevant_sections: [CoW s.18, s.19, s.20, s.21, s.22, s.23, s.24, s.25, s.45, s.50, s.57, s.59, s.60; Central Rules r.13–20, r.51–52]
applicable_acts: [Code on Wages, 2019; Code on Wages (Central) Rules, 2026 (Central sphere)]
source_references: [DEDUCTION-001 to DEDUCTION-011, WAGE-016, WAGE-017, WAGE-018, REMEDY-004, REMEDY-005]
authority_level: non_authoritative_practical_guidance
review_status: independent_research_pending_review
last_verified_date: 2026-11-08
```

### A. Situation overview
The payslip shows a deduction the employee did not expect (fine, penalty, loss recovery, notice pay, training cost, "adjustment"). Not every deduction is unlawful, and not every lawful-sounding one is allowed. The test is the statutory list, not the employer's policy.

### B1. Legal quotations (fragments)
- "no deductions from the wages of the employee, except those as are authorised". [DEDUCTION-001]
- Total deductions "shall not exceed fifty per cent" of wages in a wage period. [DEDUCTION-003]
- Fines total "shall not exceed ... three per cent" of the period's wages. [DEDUCTION-005]

### B2. Legal explanation
1. **General rule (s.18(1)).** No deductions except those authorised by the Code. A payment made by the employee to the employer or an agent is **deemed a deduction**. Loss of wages through withholding increment, reduction in rank, or suspension, for good and sufficient cause, is not deemed a deduction if the employer's arrangements meet requirements notified by the appropriate Government. [DEDUCTION-001]
2. **Authorised heads (s.18(2))**, in summary: fines; absence from duty; damage to or loss of goods expressly entrusted (or money the employee must account for) directly attributable to neglect or default; employer-supplied housing; authorised amenities and services; recovery of advances (including travel or conveyance advances) with interest, and **adjustment of overpayment of wages**; recovery of welfare-fund loans; house-building or approved loans; income tax and other statutory levies, and court or competent-authority orders; PF, pension and insurance contributions; co-operative society payments; trade-union fees and Prime Minister's relief fund contributions **only with the employee's written authorisation**; and certain railway-administration losses. [DEDUCTION-002]
3. **50% limit.** The total of s.18(2) deductions in a wage period may not exceed 50% of wages. Excess may be recovered as prescribed. The Central Rules carry it forward in instalments, with recovery in any month not exceeding 50% of that month's wages. [DEDUCTION-003]
4. **Conditions by head.**
   - **Fines (s.19):** only for acts or omissions in a notice approved by the competent authority and displayed; show-cause opportunity; total not above 3% of the period's wages; none for under-15s; no recovery by instalments or after 90 days; recorded in a register. Central Rules: approving authority is the Deputy Chief Labour Commissioner (Central); notice in Hindi, English and local language; a copy sent to the Inspector-cum-Facilitator; seven days to reply. [DEDUCTION-005]
   - **Absence (s.20):** only for absence from where the job requires; **proportionate** to the absence; special rule for ten or more acting in concert without notice (up to eight days' wages in lieu of notice). [DEDUCTION-006]
   - **Damage or loss (s.21):** not above the loss caused by negligence or default; show-cause first; recorded. Central Rules: seven days to explain. [DEDUCTION-007]
   - **Housing, amenities, services (s.22):** only if accepted as a term of employment, and not above the value supplied. The Explanation to s.18(2)(e) says "services" does **not** include the supply of tools and raw materials. [DEDUCTION-008]
   - **Advances (s.23):** an advance given before employment began is recovered from the first payment of wages for a complete wage period, and **no recovery** of advances given for travelling expenses; later advances follow the rules. [DEDUCTION-009]
   - **Loans (s.24):** as prescribed. [DEDUCTION-010]
5. **Consent is not the general test.** Most heads are authorised by the Code without consent, subject to conditions. Written authorisation is required only for specific heads (trade union fees, PM relief fund). Conversely, the employee's agreement in a contract cannot make an unlisted deduction lawful, because the Code overrides inconsistent contract terms and voids waivers. [DEDUCTION-001, WAGE-018]
6. **PF deducted but not deposited.** The employee is not responsible for the employer's default (s.18(5)). EPFO remedies were not researched. [DEDUCTION-004]

### Mistakes in both directions
| Employee may wrongly think unlawful | Often lawful | Employee may wrongly think lawful | Often not authorised or needs procedure |
|---|---|---|---|
| TDS, PF, ESI, court-ordered deduction | Authorised heads (h), (i) | "It is in my contract" | Contract cannot add heads |
| Pro-rata cut for days absent | Proportionate absence deduction (s.20) | Large "penalty" for lateness with no approved notice | Fines need approved notice, show-cause and a 3% cap |
| Correction of an earlier overpayment | Adjustment of overpayment (s.18(2)(f)(i)) | Whole salary taken for a loss | Loss recovery needs show-cause and must not exceed actual loss |
| Recovery of a salary advance in instalments | Heads under s.23 | Deduction for tools or raw materials | Excluded from "services" |
| Reduced pay after demotion or suspension "for good cause" | May not count as a deduction (s.18(1) Explanation (b)) | Notice pay, training bond or target-miss recovery from salary | Not listed; unresolved (UNRES-06) |

### C. What should the employee do?
1. **Identify the exact deduction:** amount, month, label on the payslip.
2. **Get the wage slip and your offer letter.** Match each deduction line to a statutory head.
3. **Check procedure for that head** (show-cause, notice, 3% or 50% limits).
4. **Check applicability** (REF-01). Central Rules apply only in the Central sphere; State procedures may differ (UNRES-10).
5. **Preserve evidence** (D).
6. **Ask HR in writing** for the legal basis, the calculation and any notice or show-cause paper.
7. **Record the response.** If the answer is "company policy", note it; policy cannot override the Code.
8. **Escalate** if unresolved: Inspector-cum-Facilitator, SAMADHAN 2.0 (lists unauthorised deductions), or a s.45 claim (three years; Form II has a head for deductions in contravention of the Code).
9. **Safe practice:** do not simply refuse to return employer property to avoid a loss claim. Document returns.

### D. Evidence to preserve
Payslips (before and after), offer letter and policies, show-cause or fine notices, emails explaining the deduction, bank credit proof, the displayed fine-notice list if any, and the employer's registers if provided (a register of fines and deductions is required). [WAGE-016, DEDUCTION-005, DEDUCTION-007]

### E. Possible escalation routes
HR in writing → Inspector-cum-Facilitator → SAMADHAN 2.0 → s.45 claim → lawyer. Offence complaint possible. Civil suit for recovery of a deduction is barred where the Code can provide the remedy (s.57). [DEDUCTION-011, REMEDY-004 to REMEDY-013]

### F. Exceptions and limitations
- Government departments and local authorities: Chapter III exempt unless notified (WAGE-014).
- Whether statutory deductions (TDS, PF) count within the 50% cap is not settled; the text says total deductions under s.18(2) (UNRES-07).
- Notice-pay recovery from final pay, training-bond recovery and "payment by employee" demands (treated as deductions by the Explanation) are unresolved (UNRES-06).
- Rule 19 (advance recovery limit) wording is partly illegible in my copy (UNRES-02, UNRES-20).

### G. When to consult a lawyer
Deduction is large; relates to a bond, loss or alleged misconduct; employer threatens to report or sue; tied to termination; repeated pattern across staff (a union or group application may help).

### H. Example conversational response
> "Some deductions are fine and some aren't. The Code on Wages has a fixed list of what an employer can take out of pay, such as tax, PF, certain advances and loans, and fines or loss recovery done with a proper process. Anything outside that list isn't allowed even if the contract says so. Total deductions in a wage period are also capped at 50% of wages.
>
> Which line on your payslip is worrying you, and what does it say it's for? If it's a fine or a loss recovery, did they give you a notice and a chance to respond? Keep your payslips and ask HR in writing which provision they're relying on."

## PRACTICAL-05: Employer Not Responding

```yaml
document_id: employment_wage_practical_guide
module_id: PRACTICAL-05
topic: Employer refuses or ignores a wage complaint – formal routes
jurisdiction: India – Central (verified in part) and State (largely unverified)
relevant_sections: [CoW s.43, s.45, s.49, s.50(2), s.51, s.52, s.53, s.54, s.57, s.59; Central Rules r.49, r.50, Form II, Form III]
applicable_acts: [Code on Wages, 2019; Code on Wages (Central) Rules, 2026]
source_references: [REMEDY-001 to REMEDY-016, PORTAL-001 to PORTAL-003, WAGE-015, WAGE-017]
authority_level: non_authoritative_practical_guidance
review_status: independent_research_pending_review
last_verified_date: 2026-11-08
```

### A. Situation overview
The employee raised a wage issue and heard nothing, got excuses, or was refused. They want to know what formal options exist and what each is for.

### B1. Legal quotations (fragments)
- Authority "not below the rank of a Gazetted Officer". [REMEDY-001]
- Compensation "may extend to ten times of the claim determined". [REMEDY-002]
- Application "within a period of three years" from when the claim arises. [REMEDY-005]

### B2. Legal explanation
1. **Claims authority (s.45(1)).** The appropriate Government appoints one or more authorities, not below Gazetted Officer rank, to hear and decide claims arising under the Code. [REMEDY-001]
2. **Who may apply (s.45(4)).** The employee; a registered trade union of which the employee is a member; or the Inspector-cum-Facilitator. A single application may cover any number of employees of an establishment (s.45(5)); under the Central Rules, group applications are for claims relating to the same wage period or a discrimination incident (Form II). [REMEDY-004, REMEDY-016]
3. **Limitation: three years** from when the claim arises, with late applications possible on sufficient cause. This replaces shorter periods under the repealed Payment of Wages Act (the Statement of Objects says six months to two years). [REMEDY-005]
4. **Powers.** The authority can order payment of the claim plus compensation up to ten times the claim determined, "having regard to the circumstances", and should try to decide within three months. It has civil-court powers for evidence and attendance. [REMEDY-002, REMEDY-004]
5. **Recovery.** If the employer does not pay, the authority issues a recovery certificate to the Collector or District Magistrate, who recovers it as arrears of land revenue and remits it. [REMEDY-003]
6. **Hearing.** Under the Central Rules, if the employer does not appear, the authority may hear the matter without them; if the applicant does not appear without reasonable cause, the application may be dismissed. Attend hearings. [REMEDY-016]
7. **Appeal (s.49).** Any aggrieved person may appeal within 90 days to the appellate authority, a rank above the claims authority; late appeals can be admitted on sufficient cause. Under the Central Rules an **employer's** appeal is admitted only if the claim amount is deposited (PRS notes this may be beyond the Act). [REMEDY-006]
8. **Who is the authority in the Central sphere.** Notification S.O. 2452(E) of 12 May 2026 appoints Regional Labour Commissioners (Central) as s.45 authorities; S.O. 2453(E) appoints Deputy Chief Labour Commissioners (Central) as appellate authorities. I saw the gazette header through a reproduction and the officer tables through secondary reports (UNRES-12). [REMEDY-007, REMEDY-008]
9. **Inspector-cum-Facilitator (s.51).** Advises employers and workers on compliance, inspects establishments, examines persons, and may search, seize or copy registers and wage records for suspected offences. Name and address must be on the notice board (s.50(2)). Not a claims court. [REMEDY-009]
10. **Offences (ss.52–54).** Fine for paying less than due up to ₹50,000; general contravention up to ₹20,000; repeat offences within five years can attract imprisonment. A complaint may be made by the employee, a registered union or the Inspector-cum-Facilitator. Before prosecution for a general contravention, the Inspector-cum-Facilitator gives the employer a written chance to comply. [REMEDY-010, REMEDY-011, REMEDY-012]
11. **Burden of proof (s.59).** Once a claim is filed, the employer must prove the dues were paid. [WAGE-017]
12. **Civil suit bar (s.57).** No civil suit for recovery of minimum wages, deductions, wage discrimination or bonus where the sum is the subject of a s.45 claim or could have been recovered under the Code. [REMEDY-013]
13. **Employer responsibility (s.43).** If the employer fails to pay, the company, firm or proprietor is responsible. [WAGE-015]
14. **Bonus disputes.** Disputes about fixation or eligibility of bonus are deemed industrial disputes under the Industrial Disputes Act (s.46), which is a different route from an ordinary wage claim. [SETTLEMENT-010]

### C. What should the employee do? (chronological)
1. **Define the claim:** amount, period, heads (wages, overtime, deductions, bonus).
2. **Verify applicability and sphere** (REF-01, REF-03). The authority differs for Central and State-sphere employers.
3. **Gather documents** (D).
4. **Check limitation.** Three years from when each claim arose. Do not let "waiting for HR" run time.
5. **Make or repeat a written request** with a clear date. This is practical, not mandatory.
6. **Record the employer's response.**
7. **Pick the formal route** (REF-04): ask the Inspector-cum-Facilitator named on the notice board, use SAMADHAN 2.0, or file a s.45 claim; confirm which with the Labour Department.
8. **Prepare for the hearing:** bring originals, a timeline and a one-page schedule of amounts.
9. **Consider a lawyer** for a multi-month or high-value claim, or if the employer is a company that may close.
10. **Safe practice:** stay professional in written communications; do not threaten; do not publicise the dispute before advice; keep backups of your own documents.

### D. Evidence to preserve
Everything in PRACTICAL-01 to 04, plus a dated chronology, copies of each request and reply, proof of delivery (email headers, read receipts, courier slips), and notes of calls. Also keep proof of employer identity (registered name, address, GST or CIN if you can lawfully note it).

### E. Possible escalation routes
(See REF-04.) Written request → internal grievance (where it exists) → Inspector-cum-Facilitator → SAMADHAN 2.0 or a s.45 claim → appeal rights → offence complaint (s.52) → lawyer or court.

### F. Exceptions and limitations
- Chapter III-based claims do not lie for Government departments unless notified (WAGE-014).
- State-sphere claims authority, forms and fees are unverified (UNRES-10, UNRES-11).
- I found no Central Rules text imposing a filing fee on a claimant, but fee rules should be confirmed locally.
- Whether a SAMADHAN 2.0 submission starts a formal s.45 case is unclear (UNRES-11).
- The Central Rules extraction is partly garbled around a few sub-rules (UNRES-02).

### G. When to consult a lawyer
You face a hearing and the employer has counsel; the employer disputes the employment relationship; money exceeds a few months of pay; the employer is closing or insolvent; you want to pursue prosecution; you need to respond to an employer's appeal.

### H. Example conversational response
> "It's frustrating when HR goes quiet. There are a few official options and they work differently. A written request to the employer is the quickest, but it isn't binding. If that fails, the Inspector-cum-Facilitator for your workplace (the notice board has the name) can advise and inspect records. You can also file a formal claim for unpaid wages with the labour authority, within three years of when the money fell due. That authority can order payment and add compensation of up to ten times the claim, and unpaid amounts can be recovered through the Collector. The Labour Ministry's online portal, SAMADHAN 2.0, is the current portal for Labour Code matters, but I can't be sure how it works for State-run cases, so check that against your state labour department.
>
> To point you the right way: is your employer in a Central-sphere sector, like banking, telecom, ports or mining, or an ordinary private business? And how long ago did the salary fall due?"

### A. Situation overview
The employee has left, and the employer has sent (or not sent) a "full and final settlement". The assistant should help the user **separate** the dues, identify which law and deadline applies to each, and check what is missing. Never treat every component as having a two-day deadline.

### B1. Legal quotations (fragments)
- Section 17(2): wages "shall be paid within two working days". [WAGE-012]
- Gratuity: "within thirty days from the date it becomes payable". [SETTLEMENT-004]
- Bonus: "within a period of eight months from the close of the accounting year". [SETTLEMENT-011]
- OSH s.32(1)(vi): payment "before the expiry of the second working day". [SETTLEMENT-009]

### B2. Legal explanation – component table
| Component | Governing law | Timing in the text | Key conditions | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Earned wages** (salary for days worked, allowances counting as wages, overtime) | Code on Wages s.17(2), s.2(y) | Two working days after removal, dismissal, retrenchment, resignation or closure | Employee; not a Government department (Chapter III) | Clock start and "working day" unsettled (UNRES-03). |
| **Leave wages on quitting** | OSH Code s.32(1)(vi) | Before the expiry of the second working day after quitting, discharge or dismissal | **Worker** as defined in OSH; entitlement computed under s.32(1) | Ministry FAQ says a worker may encash leave to credit at separation and no maximum is prescribed. Other employees: contract or State law (UNRES-27). |
| **Gratuity** | Social Security Code s.53 and s.56 | Employer must arrange payment within 30 days from when it becomes payable | Five years' continuous service (not needed for death, disablement or expiry of fixed-term employment); fixed-term: pro rata, one year per Ministry FAQ; covered establishments per First Schedule (UNRES-14) | Computed at 15 days' wages per completed year, or part above six months; monthly-rated: monthly wage ÷ 26 × 15; ceiling as notified. Forfeiture only in the statutory cases. |
| **Statutory bonus** | Code on Wages Chapter IV | Within eight months of the close of the accounting year (April to March), extendable up to two years | Wages up to the notified limit; at least 30 days' work; no disqualifying dismissal; establishment with 20 or more persons; excluded categories in s.41 | Not a two-day item. Ceiling not verified (UNRES-15). Bonus disputes: s.46. |
| **Commission, incentives, variable pay** | Contract / policy; s.2(y) excludes commission from basic wages | No statutory exit deadline found | Ministry FAQ: annual performance-based incentives are not wages | Whether a particular commission is wages unresolved (UNRES-05). |
| **Reimbursements** (expenses) | Contract / policy | No statutory exit deadline found | Sums to defray special expenses of the job are excluded from wages | Check approvals and company policy. |
| **Notice pay** (payment in lieu or shortfall) | Contract; s.18 limits deductions | n/a | Not listed as an authorised deduction | Unsettled (UNRES-06). |
| **Retrenchment compensation** | Industrial Relations Code (read only via the Ministry Handbook) | At retrenchment; Handbook: 15 days' average pay per completed year | Applicable to workers in industrial establishments; thresholds | UNRES-16. |
| **Provident fund** | EPF provisions in Social Security Code | Not researched | n/a | Outside this guide (UNRES-25). |

### Worked illustration for gratuity (not legal advice)
Wages (SS Code definition) ₹50,000 a month; service 7 years 8 months, rounded to 8 years because the part above six months counts. ₹50,000 ÷ 26 × 15 = ₹28,846.15 per year; × 8 = about ₹2,30,769. The ceiling (reported as ₹20 lakh) is not reached. Confirm the wage definition and service count with the employer's gratuity working.

### C. What should the employee do?
1. **List every item** you believe is owed, grouped by type (wages, leave, gratuity, bonus, reimbursements, notice).
2. **Collect the settlement statement** or ask for a component-wise breakup.
3. **Check each component against its own rule** using the table. Do not apply the two-day rule to everything.
4. **Check deductions** (PRACTICAL-04) for notice recovery and other items.
5. **Check records:** payslips, leave ledger, gratuity nomination and service dates, bonus policy and past payments, reimbursements submitted and approved.
6. **Preserve evidence** (D).
7. **Write once to HR** with the list, asking for the amounts and dates for each item.
8. **Record the response and note which deadlines have passed.**
9. **Escalate by component:** wages and deductions to the Code on Wages route (PRACTICAL-05); gratuity to the gratuity authority under the Social Security Code (competent authority for the sphere; procedure not fully verified, UNRES-14); other items by contract (lawyer).
10. **Be careful with release forms.** The Code voids waivers of Code on Wages dues, but a signed release can complicate other claims. Read before signing and consider advice.

### D. Evidence to preserve
Appointment letter with benefit terms, last payslips, leave balance statement, bonus or incentive policy and last payouts, gratuity nomination form, service dates and designations, reimbursements with receipts and approvals, resignation and acceptance emails, F&F statement, emails fixing payment dates, and bank statements.

### E. Possible escalation routes
By component: Code on Wages claim route (wages, deductions, minimum bonus); gratuity authority (gratuity); IR Code or industrial dispute route (bonus eligibility disputes, retrenchment); contract or civil claim (variable pay, reimbursements, notice pay). Inspector-cum-Facilitator and SAMADHAN 2.0 for the Code on Wages items.

### F. Exceptions and limitations
- Gratuity does not extend to persons holding Government posts who are governed by other gratuity rules. Gratuity applicability thresholds and the fixed-term one-year rule have unverified details (UNRES-14).
- Bonus excludes many categories (for example Government departments, educational institutions, non-profit hospitals) and small establishments. [SETTLEMENT-011]
- Leave wages under OSH s.32 are for "workers" only; others need contract or State law.
- Handbook and FAQ statements are non-binding.

### G. When to consult a lawyer
Gratuity or bonus disputed or forfeited for alleged misconduct; fixed-term or bond issues; large notice-pay or recovery demand; mixed Central and State issues; the employer disputes your service period; retrenchment or closure.

### H. Example conversational response
> "A 'full and final settlement' is really a bundle of different things, and each has its own rule. Salary you've already earned is due within two working days of leaving. If you're a 'worker' under the safety and working-conditions code, unused leave wages have their own two-working-day rule. Gratuity runs on a separate 30-day timeline once it becomes payable, and generally needs five years of service. Bonus is paid annually, within eight months of the year ending, so it isn't on the two-day clock. Things like reimbursements and incentives usually depend on your contract or company policy.
>
> Could you tell me how long you worked there, what role you were in, and what the settlement sheet currently shows? Then I can point out which line items to check and which deadline applies to each."

# PART D – SAFETY AND TONE RULES FOR THE ASSISTANT

1. State at the start of a substantive answer that this is general information, not legal advice, and that the user's facts decide the outcome.
2. Never present an `UNRES` point as settled. Say "this is not settled in the text I could verify".
3. Never recommend a step that involves copying confidential data, accessing former employer systems, public accusations, threats, or leaving a job without understanding notice consequences.
4. Prefer low-risk first steps: gather documents, write a factual request, ask the employer for a calculation.
5. Do not draft formal notices, claims or applications. Describe what a claim is and where to confirm the process.
6. If the user may be a Government employee, apprentice, gig worker or independent contractor, say plainly that the Code on Wages timelines may not apply and ask for details.
7. If the user describes immediate hardship, safety concerns, harassment or discrimination, acknowledge it and suggest legal aid and the labour department. Do not stay within wages only.
